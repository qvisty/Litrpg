## Homage, Dungeon Crawler Carl (a few hidden nods)

**The note:** a few times across the book, plant an **intelligent, hidden reference to Dungeon
Crawler Carl** (the LitRPG series by Matt Dinniman). A small wink for genre fans.

**Hard rules, so it never cheapens the book:**
- **Rare.** A few times in the whole novel, no more. Scarcity is what makes an Easter egg land.
- **Hidden and deniable.** A Dungeon Crawler Carl reader should feel a private smile. Every
  other reader should notice nothing and lose nothing. If a normal reader would trip on it, it
  is too loud, cut it.
- **Tone-safe and period-safe.** The book is dark, realistic, 1940s. No anachronism, no modern
  sci-fi vocabulary, no breaking the war's weight. The homage hides inside something that is
  already true to the scene. It is a resonance, never a quotation.
- **Subordinate to the story.** It must also do real narrative work in its own scene. It is
  never there only to be a reference.

**Candidate vectors (oblique, to choose from later):**
- **The cat.** Dungeon Crawler Carl has a regal, imperious cat. A safehouse or trench cat,
  described with a touch of disdainful royalty, could carry a faint echo without a name that
  gives it away.
- **The system's voice.** Their System is sardonic and showman-like. Ours is colder, but a
  single ledger line, somewhere, could tilt one degree toward that wry cruelty, then never
  again.
- **The unseen audience.** Their premise is a watched, broadcast death-game. Our spine is
  already "everyone who is keeping count," beings that watch and tally. One phrase could nod to
  the idea of an audience or showrunners behind the watching, kept ambiguous.
- **A name or a number.** A minor figure named in passing, or a recurring number, that a fan
  would catch and no one else would question.

**Where to log them:** when a homage is actually placed, note the chapter and the exact form
here, so we can keep the count to "a few" and avoid repeating the same kind of wink.
